We reused PELIC v1.0 (Juffs, Han, & Naismith, 2020; doi:10.5281/zenodo.3991977), upstream commit 853e9e545cc7a78a70839c68e8ed293cc7ed9c2c. The saved selection retained original writing-class essay responses, chose the prompt with most distinct writers (ties: lowest numeric question ID), and retained the first numeric answer ID per writer. This yielded 16 documents by 16 writers for prompt 3042, all at recorded course level 3. Course level was contextual metadata, not a calibrated ability score.
The saved calculations used ldfreq 0.3.0.9002, its English tokenizer, NFC normalization, lowercase surface forms and number exclusion. Spelling and grammatical forms were retained; no lemmatization, correction or content-POS filtering was applied. Metrics were full-document TTR, MATTR with window 50, and MTLD method mtld_seq_bidir_dirmean_lt_nomin_linear_tail_v1: strict TTR < 0.72, no minimum factor length, final-token closure checks, linear residual credit (1 - TTR) / (1 - 0.72), arithmetic mean of directions.
Reporting used ldfreq 0.3.0.9003 and the from-text-to-report guide helpers. Metadata were matched by original document ID against byte-verified source files. All 16 document values per metric were reported, with document-weighted mean, sample SD and median. Unavailable and withheld counts were zero. Complete-factor counts, the advisory length flag and forward/reverse gaps were retained; gaps describe order sensitivity, not standard errors or confidence intervals. Flags did not automatically exclude documents. No reference vocabulary, group comparison, ability inference or inferential test was added.
